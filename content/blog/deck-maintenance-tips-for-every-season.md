<h2>Deck Maintenance Tips for Every Season</h2>
<p>After more than two decades in the deck-building business, I’ve seen beautiful outdoor spaces last for 30 years — and I’ve seen brand-new decks rot in less than five.<br>
The difference? <strong>Maintenance.</strong></p>
<p>Whether you have a wood, composite, or PVC deck, every material needs some level of care. Sacramento’s hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters affect decks in different ways throughout the year.</p>
<p>Here’s my complete guide to <strong>deck maintenance throughout the year</strong>, built from years of real-world experience.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Spring: Inspection and Deep Cleaning</h2>
<p>Spring is the season to wake your deck back up after winter. Start with a careful inspection before entertaining or grilling season begins.</p>
<h3>1. Inspect for Damage</h3>
<p>Walk the entire deck and check:</p>
<ul>
<li>Loose boards, popped nails, or wobbly railings</li>
<li>Rotting or splintered areas (especially around posts and stairs)</li>
<li>Rusted screws or nails</li>
<li>Soft spots where water might have sat over the winter</li>
</ul>
<p>If you spot any soft wood or movement in the railing, fix it right away. Moisture damage spreads quickly once the weather warms up.</p>
<h3>2. Clean the Surface</h3>
<p>A good deep clean removes mold, pollen, and grime.</p>
<ul>
<li>Sweep thoroughly to remove debris and leaves.</li>
<li>Use a mild deck cleaner or a 1:4 vinegar-to-water mix for natural cleaning.</li>
<li>For wood decks, use a soft-bristle brush — avoid pressure washing unless you know what you’re doing; too much pressure can damage the grain.</li>
<li>For composite decks, use a manufacturer-approved cleaner to remove dirt and mildew.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Pro Tip:</strong> Clean your deck on a cloudy day — direct sun dries soap too fast and leaves residue.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>3. Reseal if Needed</h3>
<p>If you have a wood deck, spring is a great time to reseal or restain it. Test by sprinkling water:<br>
If it soaks in rather than beads up, it’s time for a new coat of sealer.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Summer: Protect and Enjoy</h2>
<p>Summer heat and intense UV exposure can take a toll on any deck — even low-maintenance materials.</p>
<h3>1. Wash Off Dust and Pollen</h3>
<p>A quick rinse once a month helps prevent buildup that can fade or stain surfaces. For composite and PVC decks, a garden hose or light soap wash is usually enough.</p>
<h3>2. Check Furniture and Grills</h3>
<p>Metal furniture can rust and leave marks. Add rubber pads to chair legs and avoid dragging heavy furniture.<br>
Always place a grill mat under your barbecue to catch grease and sparks.</p>
<h3>3. Shade and Sun Protection</h3>
<p>UV rays fade both wood and composite over time. If your deck gets full sun, consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Adding a <strong>pergola or canopy</strong></li>
<li>Using <strong>UV-resistant stains</strong></li>
<li>Rotating furniture to avoid discoloration patterns</li>
</ul>
<h3>4. Spot Maintenance</h3>
<p>Keep a few matching boards and stain on hand. Fix small scratches or gouges right away instead of letting them grow into bigger repairs.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Fall: Prep for Rain and Cold</h2>
<p>Fall is the perfect time to get ahead of moisture and leaf buildup — two of the biggest threats to deck longevity.</p>
<h3>1. Clear Out Debris</h3>
<p>Fallen leaves, twigs, and dirt trap moisture and promote mold.<br>
Sweep regularly and clear out corners, steps, and between boards.</p>
<h3>2. Clean Gutters and Downspouts</h3>
<p>Overflowing gutters cause water to pool around your deck’s foundation — a leading cause of wood rot and mold underneath.</p>
<h3>3. Check for Loose Fasteners</h3>
<p>Seasonal expansion and contraction can loosen screws or nails. Tighten everything before winter sets in.</p>
<h3>4. Seal the Wood</h3>
<p>If you didn’t seal in spring, do it now. Late fall sealing helps prevent moisture absorption before the rainy season arrives.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Pro Tip:</strong> Use a clear, water-repellent sealer if you like your deck’s natural color — or a semi-transparent stain for UV protection.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2>Winter: Protect from Seasonal Moisture</h2>
<p>Even if you don’t spend much time outside in winter, this is when most hidden damage starts.</p>
<h3>1. Keep It Clean</h3>
<p>Sweep leaves and debris before storms so water can drain freely. Avoid leaving non-breathable rugs or covers directly on deck boards for long periods.</p>
<h3>2. Cover Furniture and Accessories</h3>
<p>Moisture trapped under patio furniture or rugs can discolor or warp boards. Either store everything indoors or use breathable, waterproof covers.</p>
<h3>3. Inspect After Heavy Rain</h3>
<p>After storms, look under the deck for standing water, clogged drainage paths, loose flashing, or soil movement around posts. Early detection saves major repair costs later.</p>

<h2>Composite vs. Wood Maintenance</h2>
<p>If you’re wondering whether composite decking is worth the investment — it depends on how much time you’re willing to spend maintaining your deck.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Feature</th>
<th>Wood Deck</th>
<th>Composite Deck</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Maintenance</td>
<td>High (stain every 2–3 years)</td>
<td>Low (wash occasionally)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Durability</td>
<td>10–15 years</td>
<td>25–30 years</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Resistance to Mold/Insects</td>
<td>Moderate</td>
<td>Excellent</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Annual Care Time</td>
<td>20–30 hours</td>
<td>5–10 hours</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Wood decks need more care, but they have that classic charm many homeowners still love. Composite is ideal if you prefer a low-maintenance lifestyle and consistent appearance.</p>
<hr>
<h2>When to Call a Professional</h2>
<p>Some maintenance tasks, like deep cleaning or re-sealing, can be done on your own.<br>
But call a <strong>professional deck contractor</strong> if you notice:</p>
<ul>
<li>Soft, spongy boards</li>
<li>Loose or unstable railings</li>
<li>Cracked joists or sagging beams</li>
<li>Fasteners pulling out</li>
<li>Mold or rot spreading under the surface</li>
</ul>
<p>A professional inspection every few years helps identify structural issues early and keeps your deck safe and sound.</p>
