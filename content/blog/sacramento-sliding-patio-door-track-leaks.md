## Do not force a sliding door that has become hard to move

A sliding patio door may drag because its track is packed with grit, its rollers have worn, or the frame has shifted. Forcing it can damage the track or lock. Start with a careful visual inspection while the door is supported and operating normally. Keep children clear of the opening, and leave heavy panel removal to a qualified installer.

Vacuum loose debris from the accessible track and check the manufacturer's cleaning instructions. Look for flattened rollers, uneven gaps around the panel, a lock that no longer aligns, or a threshold that has visibly moved. A roller adjustment may help an otherwise sound assembly; a badly damaged track or frame needs a different scope.

## A wet threshold needs its own diagnosis

Water visible near the track does not prove the glass is leaking. Observe whether it appears after rain, sprinkler use or washing the deck. Inspect the sill drainage paths for debris without sealing designed drainage openings. Look at the exterior trim, adjacent siding and the transition to the deck. A door installed without an effective sill pan or properly integrated flashing can admit water into the wall even if the panel itself is sound.

Document where water first appears and photograph any soft flooring or stained trim. ## Questions before replacing the assembly

- Can the rollers, weatherstripping or glass unit be serviced in the existing frame?
- Is the opening square, and is any surrounding wood damaged?
- How will the sill pan, flashing and water-resistive barrier connect to existing siding?
- Will the deck or paving height affect threshold drainage and safe access?
- What safety glazing, locking and local permit requirements apply to this opening?
