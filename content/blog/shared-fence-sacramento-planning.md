## The old fence is not automatically the legal boundary

An existing fence may have been placed for convenience, shifted during earlier work or built without a current survey. A contractor can measure and build a fence, but does not establish a legal property line. If location is uncertain or disputed, resolve it with appropriate title records and a qualified survey professional before demolition.

California Civil Code Section 841 addresses adjoining landowners and boundary fences, but its notice, benefit and cost-sharing rules are fact-specific. Review the [current statute](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=841.&lawCode=CIV) and obtain legal advice when there is a dispute; a construction estimate is not a legal determination.

## Agree on the visible design from both sides

A “good-neighbor” fence commonly alternates or balances the finished appearance, but the phrase does not define one universal assembly. Agree in writing on height, board orientation, cap, trim, stain, lattice, post material and which side receives gates or hardware. HOA rules or local zoning may affect the design, especially in front or street-side yards.

## Plan access before pricing labor

Ask how crews will reach the fence line, where demolished material will leave the property and who temporarily moves plants, sheds or irrigation. Narrow side yards, pools and finished landscaping can change the method and cost. Mark private utilities and irrigation; use the required utility-location process for public underground lines before digging.

## Define the gate and grade conditions

Record gate opening width, swing direction, latch position and ground clearance. On a slope, decide whether the fence top will step or follow grade. If the existing gate drags, use the [gate sagging diagnosis guide](/blog/sacramento-fence-gate-sagging/) to determine whether the gate, post or ground is actually responsible.

## A useful written scope includes

- Confirmed fence alignment and total length
- Material, post spacing and post/footing specification
- Finished height and grade transitions
- Gate dimensions, hardware and access needs
- Demolition, haul-away and protection of landscaping
- Stain or sealer responsibility and timing
- Permit, HOA and neighbor coordination responsibilities

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a contractor decide who pays for a shared fence?

No. A contractor can price a defined construction scope. Ownership, notice and cost-sharing questions should be resolved by the owners with appropriate legal guidance.

### Should both neighbors approve the same proposal?

A shared written understanding of alignment, materials, access and payment reduces confusion. The contracting and payment arrangement should be explicit before scheduling.
