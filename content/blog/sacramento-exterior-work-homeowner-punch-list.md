**A useful exterior punch list is a short, written record of work that is incomplete, damaged or not consistent with the agreed scope—not a new wish list.** Walk the project with the contract, approved change orders and selections in hand. Number each item, identify its exact location, attach a photo, state the expected correction and assign a follow-up date.

That process is different from a seasonal condition survey. Our [Sacramento fall exterior walk-through](/blog/sacramento-fall-exterior-walk-through/) helps homeowners document an existing house before repairs are planned. This guide is for the end of contracted deck, fence, siding, window or sliding patio-door work.

## Prepare before the walkthrough

Do not begin with blue tape and memory alone. Assemble the documents that define what “complete” means:

- Signed contract and original scope
- Approved drawings, specifications and finish selections
- Written change orders, including credits and deleted work
- Permit number and inspection record when a permit applied
- Manufacturer care documents already provided
- Progress photos, especially of details now concealed
- A phone or camera and a simple numbered note

Schedule the walkthrough in daylight and, if practical, after the site has been cleaned enough to see finished surfaces. Wet siding, harsh glare and construction debris can hide or exaggerate appearance differences. Operate gates, windows and doors under normal use; do not load-test a railing, climb a roof or dismantle finished work.

## Separate four kinds of observations

The clearest punch lists classify the issue before assigning a fix.

### Incomplete contracted work

Examples include missing trim, an uninstalled gate latch, an unfinished board end or paint touch-up that the written scope included. Cite the contract section or drawing rather than writing “not done right.”

### Damage or deficient work

Record the location and observable condition: “rear deck, north guard post moves during normal hand contact” is more useful than “railing bad.” Avoid diagnosing the hidden cause unless it has been verified.

### New owner request

A new shelf, extra gate, added light or expanded paint area is not automatically a correction. Mark it separately for pricing and written approval. California’s Contractors State License Board says changes to price or scope must be documented through a written change order signed before the changed work proceeds.

## Walk the project in a fixed sequence

Start at the primary entry or work access point and move around the property in one direction. Use the same sequence at the correction walkthrough so no item disappears from the list.

### Site protection, cleanup and restoration

Check contracted debris removal, fastener pickup, temporary protection removal and restoration of areas the crew used. Test affected irrigation zones with the owner when irrigation work or protection was part of the scope. Confirm that material, packaging and temporary fasteners have not been left in planting beds, roof gutters or drainage paths.

Cleanup expectations should come from the agreement. CSLB advises that a home-improvement contract identify cleanup and debris removal rather than leaving those responsibilities assumed.

### Decks and railings

For [Sacramento deck work](/deck-builder-sacramento/), walk the normal route from the door to stairs and yard. Observe board alignment, visible fastening, picture-frame transitions, fascia, stair edges and contracted finish work. Operate any gate normally. Look for sharp edges, loose-looking components and water trapped by debris.

Do not treat a homeowner walkthrough as a structural inspection. If a guard, stair, post or support moves unexpectedly, restrict access and request prompt evaluation instead of pushing harder to “test” it.

### Siding, trim and exterior paint

For [siding replacement](/siding-sacramento/), review each elevation from a distance before inspecting accessible details. Confirm the specified profile, color, trim and transition work. Record damaged boards, unsealed-looking penetrations, finish omissions and areas where the scope called for restoration.

Some gaps and weeps are part of drainage. Compare questionable details with the product instructions and approved assembly before adding caulk.

### Windows and sliding patio doors

At each opening included in the work, operate the sash or sliding panel, lock it, check screens and review interior and exterior finish boundaries. Confirm that labels, stickers and protective film intended for removal are gone without damaging required product information.

Note rubbing, incomplete latching, visible finish damage or air and water concerns by opening number. A punch-list observation does not prove whether the cause is the sash, frame or installation; use the [window air-leak diagnostic guide](/blog/sacramento-window-air-leak-sash-frame-installation/) when airflow is the concern.

### Fences and gates

For [Sacramento fence projects](/fence-contractor-sacramento/), sight along each run and operate each gate once. Review agreed height transitions, board or panel condition, hardware, latch operation and cleanup at posts. Confirm that a gate clears the intended surface without being forced.

If grading, irrigation or a retaining wall is outside the fence scope, do not silently add that work to the correction list. Record the interface and decide whether it is a contract item, a concealed condition or a new request.

## Write every punch-list item so it can be closed

Use one row per observation:

| Field | Useful example |
| --- | --- |
| Item number | 07 |
| Location | Rear deck, north stair, second tread |
| Observation | Exposed fastener head at walking surface |
| Scope reference | Deck scope, fastening section |
| Agreed action | Contractor to reset or replace per system instructions |
| Responsible party | Contractor |
| Target date | Agreed calendar date |
| Closeout evidence | Follow-up photo and owner walkthrough |

Avoid combining unrelated items into “finish deck.” A specific line can be assigned, corrected and verified. Keep the original number when an item is reopened.

## Contractor walkthrough and permit final are not the same

A contractor-owner walkthrough checks the agreed scope. A jurisdictional inspection checks work within the permit against applicable requirements. One does not replace the other.

The City of Sacramento says a permitted project is not approved until required inspections are completed and accepted. Its permit-status guide defines **Finaled** as approval of all required inspections. If a permit applied, record the permit number and confirm its actual status rather than relying only on “inspection scheduled” or a verbal statement that the job passed.

Requirements depend on the property address and authority having jurisdiction, so use the correct permit portal.

## Assemble the closeout package

Before the project file is archived, collect the documents that apply to the scope:

1. Final signed contract and every change order
2. Final invoice showing approved additions and credits
3. Permit and inspection evidence when applicable
4. Product names, colors and care instructions
5. Written workmanship and manufacturer warranty information supplied under the contract
6. Before-cover and final photographs promised in the scope
7. Final punch list with each item marked complete
8. Relevant lien-release documents for the project’s payment circumstances

CSLB recommends getting offered labor and material warranties in writing. Its mechanics-lien guidance also explains releases, preliminary notices and payment records. Use current CSLB forms and obtain qualified advice when the project circumstances are unclear.

## Connect final payment to the written agreement

Do not invent a new payment rule at the walkthrough. Compare the completed work and open items with the signed payment schedule. CSLB states that progress payments cannot exceed the value of work performed and that changes to contract price require written change orders.

For material disputes or lien questions, consult CSLB resources or a qualified California professional rather than treating a blog checklist as legal advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is blue painter’s tape enough for a punch list?

No. Tape can mark a location, but it can fall off or be moved. Pair every marker with a numbered written item and a wide and close photo.

### Should cosmetic preferences be included?

Include an appearance issue when it conflicts with the agreed product, sample or finish scope. A preference that was never part of the agreement should be separated as a new request instead of labeled defective work.

### Does a passed permit inspection mean the contract is complete?

Not necessarily. The inspection addresses the permitted work and applicable code requirements. Contracted cleanup, finish matching, documentation or other agreed items may still remain.

### Should the punch list be created before final payment?

The walkthrough and payment should follow the written contract and payment schedule. Document unfinished contracted work and approved changes clearly, verify applicable inspection status, and use current CSLB guidance or qualified advice for payment or lien questions.
