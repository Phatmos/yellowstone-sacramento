Choose the exact decking product, board profile and layout **before** setting joist spacing. Then use the tighter requirement from the current manufacturer instructions, the approved project documents and the code requirements that apply to the address. A familiar number such as 16 inches on center is not a universal answer for every composite or PVC board.

This guide focuses on the spacing that supports the deck boards. It is different from sizing a joist or determining how far that joist may span between supports. Both decisions have to work together, but they answer different structural questions.

## Start with the product, not a rule of thumb

Manufacturers publish requirements for specific product lines and applications. Those requirements can change with the board profile, installation angle, stairs, commercial use or another special condition.

Current manufacturer guidance illustrates why the product must be known first. TimberTech describes 16 inches on center as a common maximum for standard residential applications, 12 inches for many diagonal layouts, and a product-specific exception for its Advanced PVC MAX boards. Deckorators likewise lists 16 inches for perpendicular residential installation and 12 inches for diagonal installation, while directing installers to the applicable installation instructions. These are examples—not specifications for an unrelated board.

Trex maintains current installation guides in its resource center rather than treating one field rule as a substitute for the selected product's documentation. Before ordering, record the manufacturer, collection, board profile and guide version in the project file.

## What “on center” means

Joist spacing is normally measured from the centerline of one joist to the centerline of the next. That is the “on-center,” or OC, dimension. Measuring only the clear gap between joists can produce the wrong result because joist width is excluded.

For an existing frame, measure several bays. Do not assume the spacing is uniform because the first two joists match. Note doubled members, irregular end bays, repaired areas and any framing that changes near stairs or the house.

The frame also needs a consistent plane. Correct spacing does not prevent a wavy surface when joist crowns, settlement or previous repairs leave the tops uneven.

## A product-first joist-spacing workflow

### 1. Identify the exact board

Write down the brand, product line, profile and thickness. A square-edge board, grooved board and specialty stair board from the same brand may not share every detail. Confirm that the planned fastener system is approved for the selected board.

### 2. Open the current installation guide

Use the manufacturer's current guide for that product, not a screenshot from a retailer or a remembered rule from an earlier project. Save a copy with the proposal and note any separate technical bulletin that applies to the fastener, stair or border detail.

### 3. Draw the board direction

Boards installed perpendicular to the joists usually have a different support condition from boards installed diagonally. A diagonal design may require closer spacing and creates more cut ends at the perimeter. Resolve the visual pattern before framing is finalized.

### 4. Detail stairs separately

Do not copy the main-deck spacing onto stair stringers without checking. Treads can have different support and overhang rules, and some manufacturers specify closer stringer spacing than the field joists. The stair drawing should show stringer locations, tread profile, risers, nosing and fastening.

### 5. Add support for borders and board ends

Picture-frame borders, breaker boards and butt joints need deliberate framing beneath them. TimberTech's planning guidance notes that board patterns and seams may require blocking or additional joists. Every board end must land where it can be fastened according to the product instructions, with required gaps preserved.

### 6. Compare the design with the existing frame

When resurfacing, remove the old walking surface so the concealed framing can be assessed. Measure the actual spacing, check the joist tops and ends, and inspect the full load path. Our [deck-framing inspection guide](/blog/deck-framing-inspection-before-resurfacing/) explains the hold point to include before new boards cover the structure.

If the selected board calls for closer support than the frame provides, the response may be additional framing, a revised layout, a different product or a broader rebuild. The right option depends on the condition and configuration of the whole structure—not spacing alone.

## Common layout decisions that change the framing

| Design decision | Framing question to resolve before ordering |
|---|---|
| Straight field boards | What is the product's maximum OC spacing for this application? |
| Diagonal field boards | Does the guide require closer spacing, and how will perimeter ends be supported? |
| Picture-frame border | Where are added joists or blocking needed for both field and border fasteners? |
| Breaker board | How will both sides of the seam receive full fastening support and required gaps? |
| Stairs | What stringer spacing and tread profile does the current guide require? |
| Resurfacing | Is the exposed frame sound, in plane and compatible with the new layout? |

This planning step also helps avoid conflicts with rail posts, lighting wire, drainage components and service access. For low-voltage work, map the routes and fixture locations before the surface goes down; see our [Sacramento deck-lighting layout guide](/blog/sacramento-deck-lighting-layout-before-boards/).

## Sacramento permit and plan-check considerations

The authority serving the actual property decides what review applies. The City of Sacramento lists decks among projects that typically require a building permit, while describing a limited exception for certain small, low, detached decks that are not attached to the home and do not serve a required exit door. A project must satisfy every condition of an exemption; appearance alone does not establish that it qualifies.

Addresses outside city limits may be under Sacramento County or another local jurisdiction. Verify the address, scope and current requirements before construction. When plans are required, the framing and finish-product details should agree rather than leaving the installer to reconcile them after materials arrive.

## Questions a Sacramento deck proposal should answer

A useful proposal should identify:

1. The exact decking manufacturer, collection and profile
2. The board direction, borders, breaker boards and joint locations
3. The specified joist or stringer spacing for each condition
4. Added joists, blocking or framing corrections included in the scope
5. The approved fastener system and edge or end details
6. How an existing frame will be exposed and assessed
7. Who handles drawings, permits and inspections when required
8. Which current manufacturer documents become part of the installation record

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 16-inch joist spacing always acceptable for composite decking?

No. It is common for some standard residential products installed perpendicular to the joists, but the exact product, board direction, stairs and special applications can require a different maximum. Follow the current guide for the selected product and the project requirements.

### How do I measure deck joist spacing?

Measure from the center of one joist to the center of the next. Check multiple bays and record irregular areas instead of relying on a single measurement.

### Can joists be added to an existing frame for new composite boards?

Sometimes, but added members do not fix decay, poor connections, inadequate supports or an uneven frame. Expose and assess the structure first, then detail any added framing as part of the complete load path.

### Do diagonal deck boards need closer joist spacing?

Many manufacturers require closer spacing for diagonal installation because the boards cross the joists differently. The current instructions for the exact board control.
