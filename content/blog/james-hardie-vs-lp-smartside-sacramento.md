## Two premium siding systems with different strengths

James Hardie® and LP® SmartSide® are often compared because both can create lap, panel, vertical and trim looks without using traditional solid-wood siding. They are not the same material.

- **James Hardie siding** is fiber cement.
- **LP SmartSide siding** is engineered wood treated and manufactured for exterior use.

The better choice depends on the home, design, installer, exposure, finish and priorities—not one universal ranking.

## Side-by-side overview

| Decision | James Hardie fiber cement | LP SmartSide engineered wood |
|---|---|---|
| Core material | Cementitious fiber-cement product | Engineered wood strand/fiber product with treatment and resins |
| Handling | Dense and comparatively heavy | Lighter and typically easier to cut and handle |
| Appearance | Smooth and wood-look textures, lap, panel and shingle profiles | Wood-look textures, smooth options, lap, panel and specialty profiles |
| Impact behavior | Rigid; edge handling and correct fastening matter | Often selected where impact resistance and workability are priorities |
| Moisture strategy | Requires correct clearances, flashing, joints and finish | Requires correct clearances, sealed cuts, flashing and finish |
| Warranty structure | Product-specific, with fiber-cement substrate coverage commonly described as non-prorated | Product-specific, commonly described as an initial full-coverage period followed by prorated material coverage |

Always read the current written warranty for the exact product and finish. Marketing summaries are not the warranty.

## Material and installation differences

### James Hardie fiber cement

Fiber cement is valued for dimensional stability, fire-related characteristics, resistance to pests and a familiar painted-siding appearance. Because boards are dense, handling, cutting, fastening and edge protection require the correct tools and methods.

James Hardie's current technical library includes product-specific instructions and identifies 30-year limited siding warranties for HZ5® and HZ10® products. Review the exact documents in the [James Hardie technical library](https://www.jameshardie.com/installation-instructions-technical-docs/).

### LP SmartSide engineered wood

LP SmartSide is an engineered wood product designed to retain the workability and visual character of wood while using treatment and resins for exterior performance. It is generally lighter to handle and can be useful for long boards and detailed trim transitions.

LP describes its SmartSide warranty as a 5/50 structure: an initial five-year labor-and-material feature followed by prorated material coverage. Read the current terms and exclusions rather than comparing only the largest number. See LP's [official warranty explanation](https://www.lpcorp.com/project-resources/blog/the-what-s-why-s-of-the-lp-smartside-550-limited-warranty).

## Finish choices

Both systems may be available primed for field painting or with factory-applied finishes, depending on the product and supplier.

Compare:

- Factory-finish warranty
- Color availability and sheen
- Field-cut touch-up requirements
- Caulk and joint instructions
- Future repainting expectations
- Availability of matching trim and soffit

Do not assume that a substrate warranty and finish warranty have the same term or coverage.

## Sacramento sun and heat

Dark siding can become hotter in direct sun regardless of substrate. Color, wall orientation, ventilation, wall assembly and finish instructions should be considered together.

For any product, follow manufacturer requirements for:

- Clearance from roofs, paving, soil and decks
- Flashing above openings and horizontal transitions
- Water-resistive barrier integration
- Fastener type and placement
- Field-cut treatment
- Joint and caulk details
- Paint or touch-up products

## Fire considerations

Wildfire exposure should be discussed using the property's actual location, local requirements and the tested assembly—not a vague “fireproof” claim. A siding board is one component of a wall system that also includes sheathing, water-resistive layers, vents, soffits, windows, decks and surrounding vegetation.

## Warranty: compare terms, not headline years

James Hardie states that its fiber-cement products carry a 30-year non-prorated limited warranty, while finish coverage is separate. LP describes a 50-year prorated limited substrate warranty with an initial five-year labor-and-material feature.

Important questions for either brand:

1. Is the warranty transferable?
2. Is labor included, and for how long?
3. Is finish coverage separate?
4. What installation conditions can void coverage?
5. What records, receipts and photos should be retained?
6. Must the product be registered?

## Which is better for your home?

Choose **James Hardie** when a fiber-cement assembly, its specific profiles and its warranty structure fit the project. Choose **LP SmartSide** when engineered-wood workability, long board formats, impact priorities or its finish options better serve the design.

The installer matters as much as the brand. Incorrect clearances, flashing or field finishing can damage either system and affect coverage.
