<p>After decades in the painting business, I’ve seen trends come and go. But 2025 has something different: it’s less about following the crowd and more about creating a space that <em>feels like you</em>, with walls that reflect comfort, character and wise design choices.</p>
<p>Here are the key trends I’m seeing this year — and how you, my neighbour, can make them work in your home.</p>
<hr>
<h2>1. Warm Neutrals &#x26; Earthy Undertones</h2>
<p>The cool greys that dominated the last decade? We’re letting those go. Design experts now say homeowners are seeking warmth — tones like taupe, greige, soft sand, or clay-tinted neutrals. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}<br>
These colours give you a timeless backdrop <em>and</em> a cosy feel. I tell people: “Pick a wall colour you can live with for ten years — make it a foundation, not just a trend.”</p>
<h2>2. Full-Room Colour Drenching</h2>
<p>Here’s something bold: painting every surface in the room the same colour—walls, ceiling, trim, even built-ins. The result is immersive, dramatic, and surprisingly harmonious. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}<br>
If you’ve ever been afraid of going dark or colourful, this is a safe way to make a statement without breaking decor cohesion.</p>
<h2>3. Rich Accent Hues Over Pastel Fads</h2>
<p>You might’ve noticed jewel-tones, deep olives, burgundies creeping back into interior paint palettes. These colours feel luxurious yet grounded. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}<br>
However — avoid making them the <em>entire room</em> unless you love the drama. Use them on a single wall, a nook, or built-ins for high impact.</p>
<h2>4. Warm White &#x26; Creams Over Sterile Whites</h2>
<p>Bright clean whites are still around, but there’s a shift toward warmer white tones—creams, off-whites, whites with a hint of warmth. Designers say ultra-cool whites feel too cold and impersonal for many homes now. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}<br>
For my clients in Sacramento, CA, this means we match paint finishes to natural light angles, wood tones, and furniture — not just “white walls”.</p>
<h2>5. Lofty Finishes - Artful Textures &#x26; Sheens</h2>
<p>Paint isn’t just colour anymore; it’s also about finish and texture. Matte finishes that hide imperfection, satin finishes that give subtle sheen, or even architectural-finish paints for built-ins.<br>
If you want style, consider how light hits your walls, how much the room gets used, and what kind of maintenance you’re willing to do.</p>
<hr>
<h3>✅ My Advice for You</h3>
<ul>
<li>Choose one trend that <em>feels right</em> for your home and don’t chase <em>all</em> the trends.</li>
<li>Test paint swatches in your space under real lighting—morning, midday, evening.</li>
<li>Work with finishes: a rich accent colour in matte can look far more elegant than a loud colour in glossy.</li>
<li>If you’re unsure: warm neutral + one rich accent colour + consistent trim/ceiling colour = winner.</li>
</ul>
