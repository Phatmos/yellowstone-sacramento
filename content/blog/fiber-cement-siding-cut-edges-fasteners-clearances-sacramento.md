**A fiber-cement siding installation should be reviewed in three connected passes: every field-cut edge receives the treatment required by the selected product, every fastener is the approved type and is driven correctly, and every lower edge or transition maintains the manufacturer's required clearance and drainage path.** Do not rely on one generic dimension or fastening rule. The exact product, profile, substrate, finish and current installation instructions control.

These details are small compared with an entire elevation, but they are repeated hundreds of times. A clean paint color cannot compensate for untreated cuts, overdriven nails or siding pressed against a roof, deck, patio or soil.

## Start with the exact fiber-cement system

“Fiber cement” is a material category, not a complete installation specification. Before work begins, the proposal should identify:

- Manufacturer and product line
- Lap, panel, shingle or other profile
- Factory-finished or field-painted finish
- Approved substrate and water-resistive barrier assembly
- Fastener type and attachment schedule
- Instructions for cut edges, joints and touch-up
- Required clearances at each site condition

James Hardie's current technical library, for example, separates instructions by product and climate zone and also publishes technical information for fastening, cutting, drainage and finishing. That is why an installer should save the current document for the exact product rather than work from a remembered rule or an unrelated online diagram.

## 1. Treat field-cut edges as part of the installation

Factory edges arrive with the product's intended finish. Field cuts expose a new edge at corners, openings, penetrations, gables and short end pieces. The crew should know which cuts require primer, paint, an approved touch-up coating or another treatment before those edges are hidden.

### Build an edge-treatment station

A practical workflow is to place the approved coating and applicator beside the cutting station. The installer treats the edge before passing the board to the wall. This is easier to verify than trying to find concealed cuts after installation.

The scope should answer:

1. Which coating is approved for the selected siding and finish?
2. Are cut ends treated before installation or only where visible?
3. How are factory-finished surfaces touched up without creating large, noticeable patches?
4. Who checks edges around windows, doors, utility blocks and roof lines?

Do not assume that caulk replaces cut-edge treatment. Edge coating, joint design, flashing and sealant perform different jobs. A joint may need an approved gap, joint flashing or sealant even when the adjacent cut edge has already been treated.

## 2. Check fastener selection and drive depth

Fasteners must be compatible with the product, substrate and exposure. The current instructions may specify nail type, shank and head dimensions, corrosion resistance, penetration, placement and spacing. Those choices should appear in the proposal or submittal, not be left to whatever box is closest on the truck.

### Snug is different from overdriven

The fastener head should seat as the product instructions show. A head driven below the siding surface can reduce the material held beneath the head and damage the face. A proud fastener can interfere with the next course or remain visibly unfinished.

When pneumatic tools are used, the crew should test pressure and depth on representative material and substrate. Framing condition can vary across an older wall, so a setting that works in one area should still be checked elsewhere. Individual fasteners that do not seat correctly need the manufacturer's approved correction—not a smear of caulk over a crushed spot.

### Keep fasteners away from vulnerable edges

Fastener position matters near plank ends, corners and openings. Nailing too close to an edge can crack the material; moving the fastener casually can miss the intended framing or attachment zone. Short pieces and narrow strips deserve special attention because they concentrate cuts and fasteners in limited space.

A useful field review photographs one typical fastener pattern, one outside-corner or trim termination, and representative conditions around openings before the next layer conceals them.

## 3. Preserve clearance and drainage

Fiber-cement siding is not intended to be buried in soil, pressed against a roof or allowed to sit continuously in water. The required separation varies by product and condition, so the crew should mark each type of transition on the elevation before installation.

Review at least these locations:

- Soil, mulch and planting beds
- Concrete walks, patios and steps
- Deck boards and deck-to-wall transitions
- Roof surfaces and roof-to-wall intersections
- Horizontal flashing, band boards and ledges
- Gutters, downspouts and kick-out flashing
- Window, door and utility penetrations

The gap must remain functional after landscaping, paving, roofing and trim are complete. If an existing patio or raised planting bed leaves too little space, the proposal should state whether the adjacent surface changes, the wall detail changes, or another approved solution is used. Hiding the conflict behind a lower starter course does not resolve it.

## Do not caulk a designed drainage opening

Sealant is useful only where the selected system calls for it. Some gaps must remain open so incidental water can leave the assembly. Horizontal flashing should direct water outward, and its drainage edge should not be bridged by siding, paint or a continuous bead of sealant.

At windows and doors, review head, jamb and sill conditions separately. At roof intersections, confirm step flashing and kick-out flashing before the siding closes the wall. ## A three-stage field-review plan

### Before the first course

Confirm the selected product documents, substrate condition, water-resistive barrier, flashing, starter detail and planned lower clearances. Photograph any existing patio, deck or grade conflict before it is covered.

### During installation

Check the cut station, approved edge treatment, representative fastener type and seating, course layout, joints and clearance gauges. Review difficult conditions early, while the crew can repeat an approved solution across the remaining walls.

### Before closeout

Walk every elevation from both directions. Inspect lower edges, roof intersections, horizontal flashing, penetrations and visible fasteners. Save product labels, current instructions, touch-up information, permit documentation and concealed-work photographs with the warranty records.

## What a Sacramento proposal should specify

A fiber-cement siding proposal should state:

| Scope item | Written detail to request |
|---|---|
| Product | Manufacturer, line, profile, finish and exposure |
| Field cuts | Approved treatment and when it is applied |
| Fasteners | Type, corrosion resistance, substrate and installation method |
| Clearances | Product-specific requirements at every site condition |
| Joints | Gap, flashing, sealant or accessory used at each condition |
| Existing conflicts | Plan for low grade, paving, decks, roofs and irrigation |
| Documentation | Photos, product records and closeout information |

The City of Sacramento currently lists siding as permit-required work and provides residential siding application bundles. Like-for-like work, material changes and historic properties can require different planning documentation. Confirm the authority for the property's address and the current submission path before work begins; do not assume a Sacramento mailing address identifies the jurisdiction.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every cut edge of fiber-cement siding need paint or primer?

Follow the current instructions for the exact product and finish. Field-cut requirements can differ, and a factory-finished board may have a specific approved touch-up method. The treatment should be planned at the cutting station, not guessed after installation.

### Should fiber-cement fastener heads be buried below the surface?

No. Overdriving can damage the face and reduce the material captured beneath the head. The approved fastener should be seated as shown in the product instructions, with misdriven fasteners corrected by an approved method.

### Can siding touch a concrete patio if it is painted?

Do not assume paint makes that condition acceptable. Maintain the product-specific clearance and drainage detail above concrete or other horizontal surfaces. Where the existing geometry conflicts, define a correction before installation.

### Should the gap above horizontal flashing be caulked?

Not automatically. Some flashing transitions require an open drainage gap. The product and assembly details should identify which joints receive sealant and which must remain open.
